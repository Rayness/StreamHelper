# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Streamers on Twitch, mostly Russian-speaking, often solo with no moderator team. During a broadcast StreamHelper sits full-screen on a second monitor next to OBS. The streamer glances at it between game moments: they read chat, see who followed or donated, hit a quick button, or spin the wheel for viewers. Setup (alerts, overlays, bot commands) happens off-air, with time to explore.

## Product Purpose

One desktop app that replaces the usual pile of web dashboards: OBS overlays (alerts, chat, goals, timers, banners, labels), a chat bot, the stream control panel, donations (DonationAlerts, Streamlabs), interactive games for chat (wheel, polls, giveaways, anime quiz) and the Kawaki integration. Success means the streamer sets it up once and never has to alt-tab away from OBS and the StreamHelper panel while live.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, dependable, quick. A tool that stays calm and out of the way on air (think Linear or Raycast): one accent color, dense but orderly, never flashy. Fun and personality belong on stream, in the overlays viewers see, not in the control panel.

## Anti-references

- Streamlabs / StreamElements dashboards: ad-like upsells, marketing banners inside the tool, settings buried five clicks deep.
- Gamer "RGB" UI: neon gradients, glow everywhere, animated backgrounds.
- A settings dump: endless forms with no preview of what viewers will actually see.

## Design Principles

- **Glanceable on air.** Anything needed while live (chat, events, quick actions, wheel and poll controls) is readable at a glance and reachable in one click or a hotkey.
- **Show what viewers see.** Every overlay setting sits next to a live preview; test buttons everywhere.
- **Setup is guided, not documented.** Empty states and checklists teach the next step; one click adds an overlay to OBS.
- **The tool is quiet, the stream is loud.** Restraint in the panel, expressiveness in the overlays.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast for text. Full keyboard use (command palette, global hotkeys). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` in the app; overlays follow the streamer's choices.
